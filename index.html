Academic Research

M-Health Acceptance Research

CategoryAcademic Research

RoleJunior Researcher

Research AreaInformation Systems & Digital Health

1. Project Overview

This research investigates the acceptance of Mobile Health (M-Health) technology among Indonesian citizens, with a particular focus on the moderating role of e-health literacy.

The study explores factors influencing individuals' acceptance of digital healthcare services and examines how their ability to access, understand, and evaluate electronic health information relates to technology acceptance.

The research involved collaboration with a research team and senior lecturers at BINUS University.

2. Research Objectives

  • To investigate the acceptance of M-Health technology among Indonesian citizens.
  • To examine factors influencing the adoption of mobile health applications.
  • To analyze the moderating role of e-health literacy.
  • To provide research insights into the acceptance of digital healthcare technology in Indonesia.

3. My Responsibilities

As a Junior Researcher, I contributed to several stages of the research process:

  • Research Preparation: Assisted in developing the research framework and preparing the research instruments.
  • Data Collection: Participated in collecting and processing data from 216 respondents.
  • Data Processing: Organized and prepared collected data for statistical analysis.
  • Data Analysis: Assisted in analyzing research data using SmartPLS.
  • Research Evaluation: Contributed to evaluating measurement models and structural model results.
  • Documentation: Prepared research documentation and contributed to academic report writing.

4. Research Methodology

The study applied a quantitative research approach using survey-based data collection.

The collected data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling - Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) through SmartPLS.

Key analytical procedures included:

  • Measurement Model Assessment
  • Construct Validity and Reliability
  • Path Coefficient Analysis
  • R-Square Evaluation
  • Bootstrapping Analysis

Academic Research & Publication

Evolution of Cyber Threats: The Critical Role of Antivirus

CategoryAcademic Research & Publication

RoleResearch Author & Presenter

ConferenceInternational Conference on Wireless and Telematics (ICWT)

Publication DateJuly 3, 2025

Research Summary

This study combines PMT and UTAUT to examine how security perceptions and technology acceptance relate to antivirus adoption and protective behavior.

Key findings: Response efficacy, performance expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions showed significant relationships in the proposed model. Behavioral intention was associated with protective behavior and antivirus system usage.

Data: 277 valid responses from 372 collected surveys.

1. Project Overview

This research explores the evolution of cyber threats and examines the critical role of antivirus software in addressing contemporary digital security challenges.

The study integrates Protection Motivation Theory (PMT) and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) to investigate the factors influencing individuals' motivation to adopt and use antivirus protection.

Using a quantitative research approach, the study examines how security-related perceptions and technology acceptance factors contribute to individuals' cybersecurity behavior and antivirus usage.

2. Research Objectives

  • To investigate the evolution of cyber threats and the importance of antivirus software in addressing digital security challenges.
  • To examine the factors influencing individuals' motivation to adopt cybersecurity protection.
  • To integrate PMT and UTAUT to understand cybersecurity-related behavior and technology acceptance.
  • To analyze the relationship between security perceptions, technology acceptance, and antivirus usage.
  • To identify key factors that encourage individuals to adopt protective cybersecurity practices.

3. My Responsibilities

As a research contributor and author, I participated in the research process, from developing the research framework to analyzing data and preparing the scientific manuscript.

My responsibilities included:

  • Conducting literature reviews on cybersecurity threats, antivirus technology, PMT, and UTAUT.
  • Supporting the development of the conceptual research framework and hypotheses.
  • Participating in survey preparation and quantitative data collection.
  • Processing and evaluating research datasets to ensure data quality and consistency.
  • Analyzing research findings and interpreting relationships between research variables.
  • Contributing to the preparation, writing, and refinement of the scientific manuscript.
  • Collaborating with research team members to communicate and present the findings at an international academic conference.

4. Research Methodology

The study employed a quantitative research approach using a survey-based methodology to examine factors influencing antivirus adoption and cybersecurity-related behavior.

The research combined two theoretical frameworks:

Protection Motivation Theory (PMT)

Applied to examine how individuals' security perceptions and protective motivations influence their intention to adopt cybersecurity measures.

Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)

Used to investigate technology acceptance factors associated with individuals' intention to adopt and use antivirus software.

Data Collection

Collected 372 survey responses, with 277 valid responses retained for further analysis.

Quantitative Data Analysis

Applied statistical evaluation to assess measurement quality, relationships between variables, and the proposed research framework.

Model Assessment

Conducted reliability and validity assessments, multicollinearity evaluation, path coefficient analysis, and bootstrapping to examine the research hypotheses.

5. Data Analysis & Key Findings

The research analyzed 277 valid survey responses using a quantitative approach to evaluate the proposed PMT and UTAUT framework.

The analysis covered:

  • Measurement model evaluation through factor loadings, Composite Reliability (CR), and Average Variance Extracted (AVE).
  • Multicollinearity assessment using Variance Inflation Factor (VIF).
  • Structural model evaluation through path coefficients and R-squared.
  • Hypothesis testing using bootstrapping procedures.

Key Findings

The findings indicated that behavioral intention played an important role in driving protective behavior and antivirus system usage.

Several factors, including response efficacy, performance expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions, demonstrated significant relationships in the research model.

Meanwhile, perceived vulnerability and self-efficacy were not statistically significant in certain hypothesized relationships.

These findings highlight the importance of both security-related motivation and technology acceptance factors in understanding antivirus adoption.
